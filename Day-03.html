这是昨天那段对话的续集。昨天的 Maya 把一笔可疑的拆分交易上报了；今天她回到工位，手里有 41 条积压警报，要跟组长 Tom 决定哪些该升级。这段材料里的每一个术语，都会出现在你今天的 100 词里。

Maya: Tom, I've closed out the Birmingham file. The underlying invoices came back and everything ties. No SAR.
Tom: Good. So that's one alert cleared. What's sitting in the queue?
Maya: Forty-one alerts, and eleven of them are aged — past our five-day target. Most are threshold breaches on the same three accounts.
Tom: The same three accounts? That's the bit that worries me. Are they all on the same customer risk rating?
Maya: Two are medium, one is high. The high one is the import-export client I flagged last week.
Tom: Right. So is there anything unusual in the activity itself, or is it just volume?
Maya: Volume, mostly. But there's one red flag. The high-risk account received a payment from a company registered in the same jurisdiction as its supplier — and the invoice number looks like it's been reused.
Tom: A reused invoice number. That's a classic trade-based money laundering typology, so we can't just triage it out as a false positive.
Maya: Agreed. So what would you like me to do — escalate straight to the MLRO, or run it past the second line first?
Tom: Escalate it. Draft an escalation memo this afternoon, and make sure you document the invoice reuse with the exact reference numbers.
Maya: Understood. And in the meantime, do we need to put in a DAML request?
Tom: Not yet. A DAML request only comes in once we've taken the decision to file. For now, flag the account so that nothing is released automatically, and log the decision on the file.
Maya: Will do. One more thing — if we do file, am I allowed to tell the relationship manager?
Tom: No. Telling anyone outside the reporting chain carries a tipping-off risk. Keep it to the MLRO and me.
Maya: Noted. I'll send the memo by four.
